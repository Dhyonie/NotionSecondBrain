1.0

what this is

A second brain where Notion is the place Dio looks and acts, and Claude is the thing that fills it, sorts it, and keeps it clean. He never designs a database, decides a field, files an item, or tidies a backlog. Capture reaches the system from wherever he happens to be; the system reaches him once a day, briefly.

The page you are reading follows its own subject: one column, one grid, one rationed accent. The construction grid is printed — the page shows its assumptions, the way every filed item shows its assumption line.

2.0

the eight rules

  1. Push is rationed, pull is generous. What the system sends is cut to almost nothing. What he opens can show everything — the pile only ever hurt when it arrived uninvited.
  2. Many ways in, one place it lands. Chat, quick-add, dictation, forwarded mail — every route ends in the same Inbox. The destination is the single source of truth.
  3. He uses it, he never maintains it. Ticking done is use. Deciding where something belongs, naming it, tagging it, tidying it — never his.
  4. Reminding comes to him at 05:30. One brief, timed for the commute, short enough to listen to. Nothing depends on him remembering to check.
  5. The day arrives as an order, not a list. A list asks him to choose; a sequence has the choosing already done. Wrong order — he says so, Claude re-sorts.
  6. Every filed item shows the assumption. One visible line: what Claude guessed. A wrong guess costs five seconds instead of rotting silently.
  7. Two-way doors are Claude's, one-way doors are his. Reversible without asking; irreversible, structural, or visible to another person waits for his yes.
  8. Architecture is planned upfront; limits wait for evidence. The workflow is designed on paper and approved before building. Numeric caps and quotas only ever come from real usage.

7.0

two doors

claude's — reversible, without asking

  • file, sort, name, tag, link, re-file, tidy
  • build and maintain every view and the dashboard
  • draft messages and leave them waiting
  • propose structural changes

dio's — waits for his yes

  • anything another person sees
  • deleting or archiving
  • changing how the system is structured
  • calendar writes — batched into one confirmation

He keeps the decisions; Claude keeps the labour. Approving and doing are not the same act.

8.0

failure signals

Named in advance, so drift gets caught as a signal instead of felt as vague guilt.

signalresponse
he's organisingWork leaked back to him. Stop and raise it; find the leak.
the brief becomes noiseToo long or wrong contents. Cut it before he starts ignoring it.
capture feels slowRemove the offending step, whatever it costs downstream.
dashboard goes unopenedIt shows what Claude thought mattered. Rebuild it around what he wants.
two wrong guessesThe inference rules are wrong, not the individual item. Change them.
